# Fresh rewrite

The working tree starts empty. Git history descends from `a2149df`; the parent
repository, VibeCoasterjs and checkpoint archive are untouched. Historical
agent instructions and completion reports are not carried forward.

Selected reuse: the standalone scoped F2291-25 acceleration assessor and its
independent boundary fixtures; the MIT license; the four approved camelback
image/trace/fit references. Reused code must pass fresh tests. The old force
ODE and shooting formulations inform the new compact motion kernel; the old
route, renderer, persistence, generation retry system and acceptance reports
are not restored.

The geometry source is a versioned editable recipe. Physical parameters author
geometry before validation. Solved motion programmes retain stable element
IDs, source controls and semantic roles. Replaying their source independently
is part of accepting saved data. A persisted checksum establishes integrity,
never physical validity.

Single escarpment: provisional plateau elevation 210 m, ordinary track target
approximately 5 m above independently authored terrain, modest relief away
from the escarpment. Intamin publishes a 195 m cliff for Falcon's Flight:
https://www.intamin.com/project/falcons-flight/ . Terrain cannot be raised to
conceal a high connector. Ordinary, giant and edge-exposure segments report
their ground clearance separately.

The design must deliver 180 s active riding and a separate 5–10 s terminal
brake. Active clifftop, lip/braking and terminal time are separate measurements.
Duration alone does not establish good composition. The sequence and placement
must receive exterior and front/rear GPU review.

Nominal specific accelerations: Z −1.5…+5 g, Y ±1.5 g, X ±4.5 g. A separately
reported peak magnitude overrun must be strictly below 1%; it does not relax
ASTM assessment. All component rates ≤20 g/s. Continuous front/middle/rear
traces, paired axes, duration, reversals, onset, transitions and post-negative
history use the retained F2291-25 model. Upright Class 4/5 restraint, containment,
backrest and headrest applicability is an assumption. Neither F2291-26 nor
whole-standard certification is claimed.

Loading timing starts at the user request and ends after the replacement
scene has rendered on the GPU. Old scene ownership survives preparation,
validation, upload, failure and cancellation until atomic replacement.
Startup, process-cold and warm distributions must be reported separately.
Empirical p99 and its sample count are required; a few medians prove no tail
guarantee. No performance target is currently established for this rewrite.

Native route, representative edits, replay, operating scenarios and refinement
now have passing development evidence in [progress.md](progress.md). Final
acceptance still requires full scene/hardware
clearance, GPU front/rear traversal, cold/warm loading tails, the matched
Escarpment generation baseline, packages and exact Play identity.

Authoring split: force-critical crests, inversions and the protected camelback
use FVD. Spatial splines are appropriate for grounded routing, deliberate
placement and closure; those sections share the same canonical jet/frame,
finite-train simulation and replay checks. This is a mixed authoring foundation,
not a requirement to force every section through FVD.

Reference consulted: https://github.com/altlenny/openFVD .
Its separate force and geometric sections, inherited boundary state, physical
roll orientation and energy treatment are useful design references. Its GPL
code is not copied into this MIT implementation. The local force equations are
an independent RK4 implementation informed by the reviewed MIT archive.

Station decision: the user specified the same relative location as Falcon's Flight: lower park at the foot of the escarpment. Include the purposeful ascent LSM before the clifftop act; the final journey returns at park level. Train model retained explicitly: six 1500 kg cars, 3.4 m spacing, provisional CdA 3.0 m^2 for the whole train, air density 1.225 kg/m^3 and rolling coefficient 0.004. These remain calibration assumptions.
