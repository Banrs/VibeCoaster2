# Riftwake redesign reference audit — 25 September 2026

Scratch evidence paths in this historical study now refer to D:/Coding/Codex/vibecoasterlegacy/workspace-deflation-20260925/old-probes/scratch.

This audit distinguishes published ride dimensions, a 2023 design animation, actual ride footage, consumer acceleration recordings, and the Riftwake source replay. It sets **design targets**, not a claim that a consumer-device trace is surveyed ride telemetry. Its comparison is the completed out/riftwake-completion-07 default.3 candidate. Default.3 is again the playable baseline; the specific criticisms that motivated this study remain historical context, and the later default.4 redesign was rejected.

## Source and footage provenance

- [Intamin's current Falcon's Flight project page](https://www.intamin.com/project/falcons-flight/) is the primary ride description. It gives 195 m coaster height, 250 km/h maximum speed, approximately 3 min 25 s ride time, an opening 55 m twisted drop, a 150 km/h launch to the cliff, an **outward-banked summit turn followed by a couple more turns**, a braked approach to the near-vertical cliff dive, a final launch, a 165 m camelback, and elongated return turns and hills. It explicitly describes gentle banking and drawn-out curves as force management. It embeds the [official full real POV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZiwyxnHF5hU).
- Intamin unveiled an updated 3D animation at IAAPA Expo 2023. [American Coaster Enthusiasts' contemporaneous reveal recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGDwLBX8F-A) establishes that provenance. The accessible, unoverlaid [POV/off-ride animation repost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8PQnEUjSpfA) is published by Jerez Coaster Adventures, which identifies the content as official Intamin animation; **the uploader is not Intamin or Qiddiya**, and a manufacturer/park-hosted original of this exact cut was not verified. The local copy is `out/reference-videos/8PQnEUjSpfA.mp4`. Treat the animation as intended visual form, not surveyed as-built dimensions, speeds, or G.
- The separate [CoasterTalk Falcon's Flight POV with a consumer G overlay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0UaOSBGSx20) and [official Six Flags Over Texas Tormenta POV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LesJ9BGNk8w) support phase identification, with the alignment limits below. Local retained observations are in `docs/references/falcons-flight-video-observations.json` and `docs/reference-force-audit.md`.

Sequential inspection of the Intamin-watermarked [2023 animation repost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8PQnEUjSpfA) at half-second intervals **68–86 s**, its immediate **87–90 s** cliff-dive continuation, and the separate [real consumer POV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0UaOSBGSx20) at half-second intervals **82–108 s** changes the summit reading. The animation approaches along the cliff rim at 68–72 s, then traces **one long, high, outward-banked edge turn** at about 72–76 s: the nose travels right-to-left in the external view while the train leans outward over open cliff. This appears to be a **left-hand rim carve** in train direction. It reverses across the shelf around 76–78 s; aerial frames at 79.5–82.5 s show a compact nested continuation and a low, banked traverse with **shallow local grade changes**, not a chain of full-size airtime hills. A further opposing bank/turn straightens by 84–86 s, and an aligned approach leads to the lip; the train tips into the cliff dive during 87–90 s. Intamin describes holding brakes before this drop, but their exact rail interval is not marked in the animation. The exact later signed turn hands are less secure because the animation changes camera angle. The local [sequential digital sheets](../../out/reference-videos/cliff-sequential-review/digital-sequence-1.jpg) and [real POV sheets](../../out/reference-videos/cliff-sequential-review/pov-sequence-1.jpg) preserve the inspected order.

The real POV likewise stays terrain-facing and mostly shallow through this passage: rock starts to the rider's left at 82–91 s, moves to the right during the next banked sweep around 93–99 s, opens onto a flatter shelf around 100–102 s, then comes close on the left again around 103–106 s before alignment at 107–108 s and the later brake approach. This supports **rim carve → opposing sweep → smaller alignment turn → brake straight**. The animation's camera movement and the POV's projection preclude survey-grade signed bearings or element heights. Intamin's [as-built description](https://www.intamin.com/project/falcons-flight/) independently specifies an outward-banked cliff-edge turn followed by a couple more turns and scenery viewing. Neither source supports the rejected four-crest prototype: sub-1 g readings can arise from bank, changing tangent and sensor orientation without a discrete vertical airtime hill. The passage reads slower and more scenic than the cliff dive or high-speed return, but neither footage has a calibrated speed trace; the edited POV interval and animation presentation seconds do not justify a numerical summit speed.

The same animation's **later, separate return** shows the camelback crown near 116 s, descent and valley through 118–128 s, a banked rise at 130 s, an aerial rising reversal at 132 s, and a raised banked turn leaving into descent at 134–136 s. These are visual observations, not dimension measurements. [Return contact sheets](../../out/reference-videos/falcon-thirdperson/turnaround-1.jpg) retain the frames. Its raised 180° wave/return is a distinct element; its elevation is not a reason to turn the *clifftop shelf* into repeated airtime hills.
The [retained consumer POV observations](falcons-flight-video-observations.json) identify clifftop load variation across roughly 84–106 s (selected instantaneous vertical readings 0.24–1.96 g) before the brake approach around 108–112 s; device and synchronization uncertainty prevent an exact force target for any one crest. On the **later return**, selected consumer-overlay readings are +2.44 g at 160 s, +2.11 g at 165 s, +1.56 g at 170 s, +2.27 g at 175 s, and +2.11 g at 180 s. The overlay does not identify which of these is the animation's 180° reversal, and its timeline is unrelated to the animation timeline. The RFDB Watch trace likewise has no surveyed element labels. Therefore no verified 180°-turn force floor can be extracted from these materials; the values justify an active, banked return but cannot certify element-by-element force dominance.

## Inversion timing from the official Tormenta POV

Sequential frames from the [official Tormenta POV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LesJ9BGNk8w) place the vertical-loop apex around 84–85 s and the following Immelmann apex around 90.5–91.5 s: approximately **6.5 s apex-to-apex** (visual range 5.5–7.5 s). The brief upright/banked transition between their bodies is roughly **0.5–1 s**, with no separate airtime hill or brake segment visible. Use this same-order pair to compare Riftwake's Loop-to-Immelmann spacing. The first Immelmann apex occurs around 76–78 s, about 7.5 s before the loop; the hold/drop-entry footage at 66.5–71 s is not an Immelmann apex. These visual boundaries are approximate, and source point-mass timing must be checked again against actual finite-train replay. Timestamped frames and the corrected timing table are retained in `scratch/tormenta-spacing/EVIDENCE.md`.

## Same-category size limits

The user requires each element to stay at or below **1.5 times the world record in its own category**. Use the actual labeled element's vertical extent (highest minus lowest rail point) for conservative project checks. The primary record pages do not publish every surveying convention, so comparisons near a limit need a dimension-method review. Do not substitute a coaster's overall foundation-to-track height for a loop or turnaround height.

Intamin describes Falcon's Flight's [**165 m camelback**](https://www.intamin.com/project/falcons-flight/), but the page does **not** establish a world-record category called “camelback height” or define its local base datum. It is a relevant visual scale benchmark. The prior audited ~221.5 m Riftwake camelback rise is about 1.34× 165 m, not proof of a category-record comparison. Intamin's [73 m inverted top hat on Spitfire](https://www.intamin.com/category/innovation/) is a different inversion type; it cannot replace the loop or Immelmann record. No formal primary world-record height category for a 180° wave/turnaround or overbanked turn was verified. A useful, **provisional conservative size screen** is Fury 325's built 91° overbanked horseshoe: [Carowinds' original fact sheet reproduced contemporaneously](https://themeparkreview.com/forum/topic/21598-carowinds-discussion-thread/page/157/) defines that element as a 180° turnaround, and [Carowinds' current ride page](https://www.carowinds.com/rides-experiences/fury-325?lv=true) confirms the operating horseshoe. Original August 2014 ACE News reporting, corroborated by [contemporaneous Los Angeles Times reporting](https://www.latimes.com/travel/themeparks/la-trb-fury-325-carowinds-20141027-story.html), gives it **157 ft = 47.8536 m tall**. Thus **1.5 × 157 ft = 235.5 ft = 71.7804 m**; targeting **at most 70 m** for Riftwake's labeled 180° turn/wave leaves a small margin below that comparable published figure. This is **not a certified world-record ceiling**: the 157 ft may be ground-to-crest rather than crest-to-lowest-rail rise, and no surveyed built as-built dimension was found. Compare like-for-like before claiming the user's formal category rule is proven. Fury's separately published 190 ft barrel turn is a different element and must not be substituted for the 180° horseshoe. Intamin publishes no dimension for Falcon's Flight's 180° wave/turn.

## Force evidence and redesign targets

The retained Ride Forces Database exports are an Apple Watch Falcon's Flight ride, row 7 left seat, and an iPhone Tormenta ride, row 2 seat 8, sampled at 50 Hz. The local audit projects raw acceleration onto the stored calibration vectors and applies a centered 11-sample mean, reproducing each export's stored extrema. The complete recordings' vertical ranges are **−0.986 to +3.894 g** for Falcon's Flight and **−0.518 to +4.344 g** for Tormenta. Those whole-ride extrema alone do not locate an element. The traces carry time but no surveyed distance; neither device's calibration nor phone attachment is independently certified. The Tormenta RFDB run and official camera are separate records, aligned by repeated visual landmarks at approximately video time minus 70 s with about ±2 s uncertainty.

The Tormenta windows and maxima are **exploratory**: uncertain phase boundaries can include neighboring transitions, and a scalar peak does not establish force duration or position-by-position dominance. A stronger global G peak elsewhere is not an inversion comparison. Conversely, completion-07's actual labeled inversion maxima being below the exploratory reference values on **every** seat is a practical reason to revise the force sources. Compare the new loop and Immelmann at equivalent *physical phases* and preserve genuine unloading, smooth force/roll transitions, F2291 histories, and the unchanged native project limits. Do not scale a displayed G trace or alter audit gates to claim a pass.

## Spatial redesign checks

1. Rebuild the clifftop as a **slow, shallow-grade, ground-following winding shelf** with one prominent **outward-banked left-hand cliff-edge carve**, opposing banked sweeps, and an aligned brake approach. Do not substitute a sequence of standard airtime crests: that four-crest prototype was rejected by the user and the sequential reference frames do not support it. Check both aerial and train views, with full-train terrain clearance; animation frames give choreography, not surveyed rail coordinates or speed.
2. Shrink the loop and Immelmann to their **own** record ceilings above while preserving the loop's handed crossing and the near-planar Immelmann climb/top roll. Re-solve source force histories and full-train clearance rather than uniformly scaling positions or forcing a yaw weave. A compact inversion at the same speed can raise G; this must be intentionally shaped and checked, not presumed safe.
3. Give the 180° wave/return an identifiable banked turn, elevation change, and release into the next feature. Use the provisional ≤70 m Fury 325 180°-horseshoe size screen above, then inspect the actual 3D path and per-seat loads. This does not certify a world-record comparison; Falcon's Flight's 195 m overall height is not a turnaround dimension.
4. Keep the full ride's active-to-rest rhythm near the requested ~180 s while cutting passive flat coasts and overlong braking **through authored geometry and actuator distance**, not by relabeling station/trace time. Completion-07's full simulated duration is **181.818 s** (the generation-only moving interval is 170.070 s), already close to that time target; the issue is where its activity occurs.

For acceptance, report actual post-generation element vertical extents with category/source, summit and turnaround 3D views, terrain/train-envelope minimums, front/middle/rear force phase maxima and durations, native derivative/rate checks, and independent F2291 history results. The existing completion-07 native `accepted: true` does not cover these newly requested size and visual-quality requirements.
