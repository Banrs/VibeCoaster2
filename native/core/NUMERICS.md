One ordered source itinerary owns physical geometry and ports. The fixed cliff ascent, summit, dive, fastest launch and giant camelback progression shares the route with the required loop, Immelmann and supporting airtime. Source energy uses the finite train's mean potential and losses; initial source intent is resolved against actual closed passive lengths. At most eight complete-ride builds resolve measured source, occupied-turn and motor-inlet speeds within 0.5 m/s. The first terrain assessment sizes the final footprint; subsequent feedback uses half-step relaxation. Final bank smoothing retains its 1.2-second spatial response radius at measured speed, an authoring choice rather than an ASTM limit. Full rider-offset forces, clearance and unchanged 960/1920 Hz gates decide acceptance.

CMake publishes the core's floating-point policy to every linked consumer: `/fp:strict` on MSVC and `-ffp-contract=off` otherwise. Internal geometry headers are also instantiated by component tests, so library-only flags are insufficient. A reproduced fused/unfused turn residual showed that mixing arithmetic policies can disagree at a feasible boundary. The compiler policy is shared; turn constraints and acceptance tolerances are unchanged.

Canonical generation has changed, so earlier exact-version saves require their preserved application. The previous 0.7.3 applications used 0.7.2-pacing.2 geometry; their [packaged review](../artifacts/review-v073-20260908/REPORT.md) does not verify this revision. See [delivery status](../DELIVERY.md) for the published executable. SI metres/seconds/newtons/watts and right-handed XY ground, Z up remain unchanged.

## Canonical geometry and rider forces

Each canonical centerline span is degree seven, with shared true-arc position/tangent/curvature/curvature-derivative endpoint jets. This gives G3 position at joins. The raw reference-up vector and bank are degree-five polynomials with shared true-arc first and second derivatives, fitted on the nonuniform arc grid with a stencil of up to five points. Projecting/normalizing the raw up and banking around the tangent gives the C2 orthonormal frame inside the checked nondegenerate domain. `Track::sample`, `sampleSpan` and analytic kinematics evaluate these same caches; there is no second renderer spline. Positive eight-point Gauss quadrature and safeguarded converged inversion provide the shared distance adapter. The UE boundary reflects Y and converts metres to centimetres once.

The train is constrained to that track. Net acceleration sums gravity, bounded rolling friction, drag and per-car drive/brake forces. Midpoint integration resolves zero-velocity events; bounded static friction can hold the stopped train. Exactly contiguous operation runs with identical kind, target, force/power, ramp, exit fade and stop profile are coalesced once during generation, so a continuous physical drive region has one entry ramp. Gaps, overlaps, wrapping regions and differing profiles are not merged. Each operation persists `exitFadeMeters` (default 1 m, supported [.01,1000] m). Generated fades use `max(1 m, targetSpeed × rampSeconds)`; station target zero gives 1 m. The bounded drive force multiplies its existing entry ramp by smoothstep of forward canonical distance to the physical exit divided by that fade length, including wrapped intervals. Saved operations are replayed as stored. Generated station braking begins after the complete train clears the final airtime section. Its hardware capacity is sized from the remaining return distance with a 65 m/s approach estimate; insufficient bounded stopping work rejects the candidate. The final stopping profile uses 6 m/s² service deceleration; its actual rider load, onset and complete stop remain independently assessed. Hardware force and power account for the canonical downhill grade within the selected longitudinal envelope. Deceleration and the 0.2 m stop offset persist as explicit operation fields; actual finite-train replay still decides whether the stop succeeds. This is not a suspension, articulated rigid-body, wheel-contact or flexible-coupler model.

For the physical front, middle and rear car, rider specific acceleration is `(T+h U_s)a + (K+h U_ss)v² + (0,0,g)`. Its components use the same rotating rider axes. Vertical specific-force rate is an **analytic instantaneous derivative**, including curvature derivative, frame rotation and seat offset; its peak is sampled at solver times. The field `maxJerkGps` names that vertical component rate, not world-space vector jerk. Lateral/longitudinal rates remain discrete first differences of their solver-rate force series. They are descriptive and unassessed by default; explicit finite optional limits activate their force-rate and convergence gates. Neither analytic point evaluation nor two-resolution agreement proves continuous extrema between all solver times.

## Mandatory numerical acceptance

Production generation, save and load require exactly **960 Hz**, with mandatory independent **1920 Hz** half-step verification. Other requested production steps reject without silent clamping. Standalone `simulate()` and `compareSimulationConvergence()` retain independent resolution controls for numerical investigation and tests. `Design::accepted()` checks the exact requested/coarse/fine rates as well as a valid report, completed uncancelled simulation and convergence both performed and passed. `verifyConvergence()` rejects a substituted request rate before replay. Agreement at a coarser pair cannot qualify a ride for acceptance. Generation, save and load all run this check; saved convergence flags and stored telemetry are not trusted.

Both resolutions must satisfy the selected speed, launch, reference and force limits. Convergence compares peak speed using 1% and force/exposure/assessed-rate/statistical metrics using 2%, each scaled by `max(1, abs(fineValue))`, with strict less-than acceptance. Per-seat signed extrema, means and rolling means are included. Raw modelling guards are vertical [−2.8,6]g, lateral 3g, longitudinal 6g and vertical rate 20g/s. In addition, every rider must pass the selected historical enhanced-restraint profile: signed duration, 5Hz-filtered force, 100ms onset, paired-axis loading, reversal and negative-to-positive history. Those profile metrics also participate in convergence. The [source and modelling scope](FORCE_GUIDELINES.md) distinguish this implementation from current-edition or physical ride certification. Presentation traces are nominal 60 Hz with an actual zero-speed terminal sample; the final timestamp interval can be shorter. Presentation interpolates that accepted trace rather than integrating independent physics.

## Geometry and structures

Terrain routing jointly chooses source placements, independent climb/return headings and exact closed corridor lengths. The final rigid hill chain ends on the actual canyon rim; the station has a physical straight arrival using its existing 100 m boundary reservation. Active septic height windows follow the fixed landscape while preserving source ports. Their complete sampled floors and final swept bodies remain separate checks. A powered canyon climb has a 150 km/h authoring target (Intamin's published Falcon's Flight climb context), distinct from the selected record-speed signature; actual force/power, gravity, losses and feedback determine speed.

A crossing retains a clear underpass when available. One rigid tail translation solves all forbidden separation intervals; it never warps force-authored interiors. All ordinary turns, including the braking return, use actual maximum any-car occupancy speed. Loop trimming is sized from passive work on the composed canonical approach, and a reached, stalled loop may correct its existing upstream motor within the same eight-rebuild budget. No speed is assigned instantaneously. Force-axis banking is no longer clipped at 86 degrees; signed force history, rider offsets and roll response still gate acceptance.

The active 0.8.3 planner solves seeded folded routes with a vertically separated interior crossing, a force-authored record-height hill, an ordinary full loop, a high joint-force Immelmann with descending pullout/valley return, and two force-authored airtime chains containing four supporting hills. Banked turns also vary in elevation. Station planning uses local terrain and a 16 m bay-height budget; the departure signature shares its datum and retains its source shape. The canyon climb's geometric endpoint is the following inversion inlet, independently of the earlier motor/brake operation boundary. This is a bounded hybrid route grammar, not arbitrary free-form topology. Source force inputs do not substitute for actual finite-train measurements after joining and terrain placement. The separate terrain-led itinerary remains an experiment, and source-family bounds and integration evidence are documented in [FVD.md](FVD.md).

Supports contain explicit closed tapered steel and full-radius terrain-anchored footings. Banked outreach uses the actual canonical frame while tower footprints remain horizontal. Station platforms, canopy, posts, piers and footings are canonical oriented boxes. Every member uses its maximum radius for train, track-hardware and station clearance. The narrowly bounded verified own-spine endpoint allowance never exempts train, rails, ties or station. These proportions and geometric checks do not establish structural load capacity or foundation adequacy.

`buildClearanceSweep` rebuilds and checks all position/frame caches. Restricted Bernstein hulls of the actual septic position and quintic up/bank polynomials bound speed, projected-up norm and complete frame angular rate. Every canonical-parameter cell has true arc bound≤.04 m and angular variation≤.08 rad. Body radius<4.2 m then moves<.188 m from the midpoint, covered by .20 m padding; hardware radius<.9 m moves<.056 m, covered by .06 m. A 16 m spatial index culls distant support members. There is no assumed global normalized-linear-up rate.

The terrain certificate covers the complete body: longitudinal half-length 1.275 m, half-width 1.5 m, underside −.8 m and configured rider headroom up to 3.6 m. Analytic terrain gradient bounds and a concave lower-clearance envelope cover its interior footprint, with unchanged .20 m continuous motion padding. This complete certificate is now the sole terrain-clearance gate; the older sampled cross-section's extra 1.6 m reserve is removed. The selected minimum and modeled body dimensions are unchanged. Nonadjacent central chords retain a 6 m threshold; an O(cells+chords) cursor certifies true arc≤2.1 m between actual located endpoints. With curvature≤.2, the two chord-error allowances leave>.679 m beyond body+hardware radii. The separate 12 m local-rail adjacency exemption remains an explicit contact-modelling limitation. See the [historical clearance certificate](../CLEARANCE_CERTIFICATE.md) for the unchanged continuous-bound construction and [self-track assessment](../SELF_TRACK_ASSESSMENT.md).

Conservative expanded boxes can reject separated shapes when clearance cannot be certified. UE tubes use 2 m straight tessellation between rings, so canonical certificates are not formal intersection proofs for every rendered triangle. UE compilation, cooking, actual POV inspection and GPU profiling remain required.

## Physical scale and public design references

Speed and rider loads are calculated from SI geometry, gravity, the finite train and the persisted force/power-limited operations; they are not sampled random telemetry. Seeded authoring choices vary within bounded design families and must survive final simulation. FVD crest input loads are design inputs, not measurements of the final terrain-composed train. At equal orientation and specific load, the centripetal term scales as v²/r: changing 250 to 300 km/h calls for 1.44 times the radius, not an unchanged copied element with a new speed label.

[Intamin's Falcon's Flight design account](https://www.intamin.com/products-list/lsm-launch-coaster/) informs the escarpment ascent, rim recovery and low high-speed return; its published specifications are not a force-time recording. [The Tormenta operator release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/record-breaking-tormenta-rampaging-run-opens-today-at-six-flags-over-texas-302821429.html) identifies a 218 ft Immelmann, distinct from overall coaster height and drop. The game's true half-loop/half-roll geometry is evaluated at its own speed and local-terrain height. The [force-based track mathematics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.1394) supports authoring from load histories. None of these sources supplies the missing eligible I305/Pantherian sustained-force benchmark or establishes certified rider safety limits.

## Force-designed sources and whole-route energy planning

FVD supplies four airtime sources, full-loop pitches and a joint reversal/pullout source. Quaternion RK4 integrates gravity-coupled centerline force and roll inputs; independent sampled canonical replay checks each source. Airtime shooting closes level ports. Pitch shooting closes height and a half-revolution, retaining actual displacement; a positive-load crest joins the full loop's mirrored pitches. Joint reversal shooting integrates continuous normal/lateral/twist controls to a descending upright port, leaving horizontal displacement and heading free. The pullout consumes that actual port and shoots a lower level valley without a horizontal reset or propulsion. Geometric apex height, highest inverted height, minimum speed and exit speed are separately measured. The ordinary loop's 55-68 m source height does not replace or disable the separate terrain-relative inverted-apex target.

The generator retains the chosen route family/site during up to eight energy-feedback rebuilds. Its 0.5 m/s maximum residual covers actual airtime entry speeds, full-loop apex speed and Immelmann exit speed after the roll. `AUTHORING_ENERGY` rejects unresolved candidates; the tolerance is an authoring consistency check, not a force or safety tolerance. Trim requirements and existing upstream motor supply are corrected separately for both the full loop and the reversal, using measured energy and then re-simulating bounded force/power and real drive fades. A genuine Immelmann stall may bootstrap supply from actually reached entry ports; unreached exit and later-element telemetry is never extrapolated. Bootstrap and completed-route corrections share the same eight-rebuild budget, and acceptance still requires complete replay with the full residual tolerance.

The internal passive-transfer planner transports observed train speed using mean car-height changes, drag and rolling losses over at most 2 m steps, with cancellation and explicit unreachable results. It omits all drives to estimate available coasting energy. It cannot assign runtime velocity, establish full-ride convergence or replace any acceptance check. Transition guards protect the new source ramps, but terrain placement and refitting can still alter energy and delivered forces. See [FVD equations, module contracts and scope](FVD.md); real per-element rider loads and terrain composition remain under review.

## Historical organic terrain profiles (0.6.0-organic.1)

This subsection preserves the old tanh-shelf mathematics and its original test scope. Active terrain uses the compact canyon walls described below; these old equations do not describe current cliff geometry.

Terrain now carries explicit vertical/horizontal scale, world offsets, rotation, cliff amplitude and transition width. Base defaults retain the original analytic flat/hills/canyon fixtures. Generation may resolve a default profile from its seed once; saved profiles are replayed as stored. Seeded hills vary amplitude and wavelength. Seeded canyon adds a smooth tanh shelf with a 195–225 m asymptotic plateau difference to a subdued ravine; a route only experiences the portion it actually visits. This is physical terrain elevation, not extra local-ground track height or a proven record. All canonical height queries and rendering share these parameters.

For rotated/scaled terrain, the global gradient certificate is `(baseBound * verticalScale + cliffHeight / (2 * cliffWidth)) / horizontalScale`, with conservative base bounds .056 for hills and .28 for canyon. Rotation preserves the gradient norm; the tanh derivative is bounded by one. Full-body terrain clearance, station foundation enclosure and support footings use the complete profile bound. Station footings sample their entire grid and add the unsampled Lipschitz margin. Malformed/nonfinite/out-of-range profiles are rejected before geometry replay. The current exact geometry identity requires a bounded `TERRAIN_PROFILE` extension; frozen 0.5 saves remain available through their original runtime. Profile math tests do not establish whole-ride acceptance on large terrain relief.

## Historical terrain baseline and physical entry control - 0.6

This subsection records the original 0.6 baseline and corridor implementation. The later baseline flow correction, folded planner and pacing sections above/below supersede the described baseline solver and perimeter layout. Persisted bounded-drive semantics and acceptance limitations still apply.

The terrain-following baseline now fits degree-seven spans with shared position and first, second and third grid derivatives. Fixed station controls have all three derivatives zero. This removes the former C2 baseline's third-derivative discontinuity before the unchanged canonical G3 fit. The bounded projected coordinate solver minimizes squared second differences plus four times squared third differences, with a 0.001 terrain-following term: its free-control update is `(64*(b[k-1]+b[k+1])-25*(b[k-2]+b[k+2])+4*(b[k-3]+b[k+3])+0.001*target[k])/86.001`, projected onto the existing terrain lower bound. The third-difference weight corresponds to roughly 100 m of spatial smoothing on the approximately 50 m control grid. Fixed station height and terrain-clearance constraints remain hard constraints; the bounded solve is a planning heuristic, not a force certificate.

For a non-flat seeded landscape, the generator samples the following canonical hill or reversing element's actual crest at 2 m spacing, including its endpoint. It adjusts the existing nominal entry target using `v_target² = max(20², v_nominal² + 2*g*(actual_climb - authored_height))`. This preserves the nominal ideal energy margin across terrain-induced elevation differences. The 20 m/s floor is an explicitly chosen controller target floor, never a clamp or reset of simulated speed. Real force/power-limited acceleration and braking operations share the same zone and target, with opposite demand signs; at each car only the accelerating or braking demand can act. Losses, entry/exit fades, train length and both mandatory simulation resolutions still determine actual speed and acceptance. Hill entry, the retained full-loop entry and the new Immelmann entry all use this planning rule. It does not establish a force-vector-designed circuit or a real reference record.

The final station approach removes bank through a C2 quintic fade over 100 m before an upright reserve of `max(40 m, train_half_length + 18 m)`. The fade applies to authored bank and again after replay-based bank refinement, so refinement cannot reintroduce bank into the boarding envelope. Position closure and every station/support/train clearance check remain unchanged. A failed conservative clearance certificate is retained as a rejection; it is not reported as proof of an actual triangle collision.

The new six-corridor family retains the large hill and offset full loop, and adds a genuine rising half-loop followed by a half-roll (Immelmann), plus a separately placed half-roll followed by a descending half-loop (dive loop). Independent seeded pitch/roll profiles preserve their endpoint frames. Selected recovery corridors bend inward, but a perimeter route bias remains; this iteration does not establish convincing space use or eliminate long powered recoveries.

For an intensity-driven reversing-pair layout, the extra complete helix lap is accounted separately from the internal 7550 m perimeter-corridor search bound. Its full arc remains in reported track length, simulation, clearance, support construction and renderer resource limits. This search-accounting change does not relax any user target or acceptance gate. Synthetic 20/30 g·s inputs exercise target-driven software behavior only and are never authentic I305/Pantherian benchmark evidence.

## Steep canyon terrain and local certificates (0.7.0-folded.1)

The seeded canyon now has a meandering valley floor and two steep walls leading to broad rims. In rotated/scaled profile coordinates, `w = y - 120 sin(x/850)`. The floor occupies `|w| <= 260 m`; each wall has the persisted full transition width `cliffWidth` and adds `cliffHeight * S7((|w|-260)/cliffWidth)`, where `S7(u)=u^4(35-84u+70u^2-20u^3)` is clamped outside [0,1]. Its first three derivatives vanish at floor/rim joins. Seeded wall widths are 120–180 m before horizontal scaling; floor-to-rim height is 195–225 m plus subdued base terrain. The active version changes the meaning of cliffWidth from the earlier broad tanh shelf; frozen 0.6 files are not reinterpreted.

The global wall gradient bound is `2.1875 * cliffHeight * hypot(1,120/850) / (cliffWidth * horizontalScale)`, plus the original base gradient bound. `localSlopeBound(x,y,radius)` encloses the entire world-XY disk: the meandering coordinate varies by at most `hypot(1,120/850)*radius/horizontalScale`. This interval maps to the compact wall parameter; the derivative maximum is evaluated at the point closest to 0.5 in that interval, including intervals crossing either wall join or the valley center. A floor/rim footprint inherits no remote wall gradient. This is an interval derivative bound, not a gradient sampled at its center.

The train certificate queries a radius enclosing its complete oriented body and motion padding. Station footings query their complete diagonal radius; support footings query their full radius and steel samples include the member radius plus half the inter-probe distance. Existing geometric reserves, force limits and acceptance rules are unchanged. Steep-wall placement can still fail those bounds and must be rerouted or rejected. Rendering uses the same physical height function; no extra visual-only cliff is added.
